# Ciarans Vienna to Budapest Blog

After saying goodbye to Kate I met three of my fellow cyclists at Rectory Road station at 7.45am on Saturday.  Anita and Darren I knew vaguely from volunteering at NLAH.  I hadn't met Steve before.  We discussed cycling to London Bridge to catch the train to Gatwick airport but decided to go by train.  It was quicker and we were all pretty tired because of the early start.  Despite the trip ahead of us I was the only one who hadn't had a big night out the day before.    
 
We got to Gatwick without incident and were joined by Rose, Charlie, Mark and Helen.  As most of us were inexperienced in dismantling bikes it took longer than expected to get them into the bags.  I used all the bubble-wrap I had taken from work and could have done with more.  My rear mudguard seemed doomed; oil and dirt made it impossible to remove the screws and it stuck out awkwardly in my bike bag.  It took so long to check in that I thought we were going to miss our flight but eventually it was done.  Like most people I slept most of the way to Vienna.  
 
Upon arrival I discovered my mudguard had survived but my pannier rack had been mangled.  I thought my trip was over: I would either lose the rack - and not be able to carry my bags - or be unable to attach my rear wheel.  Fortunately I was able to bend the rack back into shape.  As we struggled to reassemble our bikes in the arrival lounge of the airport men offered advice or, more often, stared unhelpfully at us.  As predicted, my hand-pump was unable to provide enough pressure to cope with the extra weight of my luggage.  Luckily Anita had a miniature foot pump that was to do a great job of getting us through the trip. 
 
After what seemed like hours we set off in the rain on the 17 mile trip to our hostel.  We made a number of stops to iron out problems with the newly reassembled bikes and to buy food.  We got slightly lost on one occasion and, perhaps because of our inexperience at this stage, Vienna took longer to negotiate than later towns and cities.  We checked into the youth hostel where the receptionist seemed disgusted that none of us were members of Hosteling International.  We were joined by Sarah and Elaine who had flown out a few days before.  Sarah's bike had been badly damaged during the flight and she was now unable to cycle.  She told us she planned to stay in Vienna and do some sight-seeing before flying home to visit family in Dorset.  
 
Vienna was pretty but dead.  Perhaps we were in a quiet part of town.  It seemed to be largely populated by unhealthy looking men in their 60s who sat drinking and chain-smoking alone.  We had dinner in an odd pub which looked like it was decorated by a madman: it was a strange mixture of Austrian twee and photos that would not have been out of place in a cheap barber shop.  If the atmosphere was strange at least the food was reasonable and the beer was very good.  Those who had dessert said it was excellent.  I went back to my room and discovered that I had a room-mate that I could smell from the corridor.  Luckily he was asleep and I was able to change rooms without waking him; I felt rather foolish explaining why I wanted to move to the receptionist but it was better than putting up with a terrible smell for eight hours.  
 
The next morning we set off on the first leg of our journey proper.  We cycled 30 miles through beautiful sunshine.  After getting through Vienna we travelled along what we initially thought was the Danube but turned out to be a reservoir.  At various points we saw water-skiers, fishermen and nudists.  Anita had a puncture which took a while to repair.  Shortly after we turned onto a cycle path that went through meadows.  After several miles we stopped and ate chicken and sauerkraut at a cafe.  Most of the clientele were hard drinking bikers.  After lunch we cycled for an hour before a light shower convinced us to turn off for a coffee.  I resisted the puerile urge to buy an ice-cream called 'Bum Bum.'  We got on the road again fairly quickly, crossed a ravine and entered Slovakia.  We stopped in the centre of Bratislava to have refreshments and then continued to our first campsite. Although not as pretty I immediately warmed to Bratislava much more than Vienna.  The centre was not unlike Austria however the feel of the rest of the city owed more to L.A.: it was full of big wide roads with billboards and revolving signs for car repair shops.  Whereas Vienna felt like a wedding cake Bratislava felt real.  
 
Our campsite was by a lake.  It looked as though it would be package holiday hell at the height of summer but for now it was quiet.  It took a while to set up my tent, having never done it before.  I grabbed a very welcome shower and managed to soak some of my clothes in the process.  Then I headed to the campsite restaurant for beer and food.  At first we worried our waitress was not very friendly.  Steve was rebuked with the line "this is not McDonalds!" when he asked for chips.  However, it turned out that this was just her shtick.  She advised us very well on portion size and when the mosquitoes started attacking us she gave up her break to rearrange the tables inside the restaurant.  We headed to bed early-ish as we had to be up by 6.30am to start the 75 mile journey to our next campsite.  That night I slept surprisingly well without either a pillow or a groundsheet.
 
The next morning we packed up our tents and headed to the huge Tesco supermarket we passed on the way to the campsite.  I looked after the bikes while the others shopped for supplies.  It was very cold and I passed the time by looking at the election posters that were everywhere and laughing at the 'no automatic weapons' sign on the supermarket door..  After shivering for five minutes I pulled on combats and hoody.  We ate breakfast in the car park and headed through the city.  I volunteered to carry one of the CBs and stayed near the back to watch for anyone having problems.  This part of town seemed tougher to cycle than the route we had taken the previous day: the roads were potholed and the traffic was busy.  At one point my sleeping bag bounced free of the bungee straps holding it in place.  Luckily I was able to retrieve it.  
 
The day quickly warmed up.  We got off the roads and on to a cycle path.  Unfortunately Mark's tyre had been giving him trouble since breakfast and we had to stop and repair it.  I tried to radio the front of the group but the reception was bad.  We ended up taking our break where we carried out the repair rather than at the designated stop.  When we rejoined the others at the intended cafe they were surrounded by flies.  Those of us who had stopped had had a lucky escape.       
 
The rest of the morning was spent travelling along the side of the Danube.  There were a lot of flies.  At first I panicked but none of them seemed to do any harm.  After a second break the flies disappeared.  Steve announced that he had brought the questions from a recent quiz in which he was the quiz-master.  We broke into two teams - girls against boys.  It was close throughout but eventually the girls won by getting a four-point question right. 
 
We carried on until we reached an unpromising looking pub in a small village.  It was now very hot.  Some elderly looking locals with bad teeth sat outside drinking and a speaker made announcements at random.  We bought all the pre-made sandwiches in the pub - which turned out to be delicious.  After lunch we cycled through the town which seemed to be filled with barking dogs (this will be a recurring theme in Slovakia and Hungary.)  
 
By the next town we had travelled 50-55 of the 75 miles for the day.  We saw a huge crane in an enormous next on top of a telegraph pole.  Things seemed to be going well but they were about to go wrong: we asked a local for directions. She was either a sadist or the village idiot but either way she directed us down a gravel path telling us that we couldn't get lost.  I was near the front of the group and was not keen on this path as I was worried about my tyres.  However I said nothing because we were in a very rural area and I assumed it couldn't last for long.  Sure enough, it quickly became a mud-track - then scarcely that.  Branches started blocking our path - then trees.  We dragged our bikes around the first major obstacle and carried on as mosquitoes started to buzz around our ears.  Eventually a huge tree blocked our path and I stopped to wait for the others.  
 
The mosquitoes swarmed around me and it became difficult to think.  I tried to dig clothes from my panniers to cover up and nearly knocked my bike over trying to swipe at the insects.  Charlie and I helped each other to cover up.  The clothes made things better but we were still in hell!  By balancing a branch on my arm I was able to create enough space for three of the others to get their bikes under the tree.  Someone then suggested we should turn back but no-one seemed able to make a decision.  Tempers were at breaking point.  Despite our predicament I hadn't quite accepted that this was the wrong way.  My instinct was to press on, reasoning that we had to be near the end of this stretch.  Nevertheless, when the decision was made to turn back I was relieved.  
 
In order to get back under the fallen tree I end up imitating the Incredible Hulk and ripping branches off.  I cycled the four kilometers back as quickly as I could without causing immediate damage to my bike.  It was at this point I realised that although the water to one side looked like the river, there was a stagnant pond to the other side which must have been the source of the insects.  I was delighted to see the gravel path from earlier.  Everyone emerged from the swamp in shock.  
 
After a few minutes we carried on by a different path.  We speculated that even if we had continued through the swamp it might not have taken us to the road.  A few miles later the derailer from Elaine's bike became badly damaged.  Steve and Darren did their best to repair it but when the derailer snapped the best they could do was give her one fixed gear to ride on.  Somehow she managed to make it through the next two days on this machine.   
 
Ten miles later we passed over a bridge and into Hungary.  It started to rain just before I started to put my tent up.  I figured if I was quick it would be fine.  Unfortunately the rain became a thunderstorm before I could get the waterproof layer on.  With help I was able to finish putting up the tent.  When the rain ended we showered and went for pizza.  It rained again on the way home and I had to improve the set up of the tent in rain.  Given the makeshift way in which my tent was assembled I was lucky not to spend the night sleeping in a puddle. 
 
After cycling 75 miles on Monday we allowed ourselves a lie-in.  We crossed the border back into Slovakia and found somewhere to have brunch.  The food wasn't very good and the service was slow but that saved us from another down-pour.  The weather on Tuesday was very unsettled: at points the skies were so blue that you could not believe it could possibly rain however the dark clouds could roll over quickly and by late afternoon we had cycled through a storm with forked lightening.  Much of the morning was spent on off-road paths.  These were slow, muddy and our bikes were unsuited to them.  I was relieved when we got on to a road.  We stumbled across a luxury spa and for ten Euro were able to go for a swim.  My legs were so covered in mud that I feared I would be refused entry.  I took advantage of the showers before entering the pool.  Anita, Darren, Helen, Mark, Steve and I sat the hot-tub and played catch in the pool itself.  The others ate dessert.  
 
Refreshed, we headed back on the road and made very good time for the next hour or two.  We cycled through a lightening storm as there was no other choice.  It was coming to an end by the time we reached the next village.  We stopped for a break and tried to dry off a bit.  We then cycled through what seemed like an industrial region.  Darren, who was navigating at this stage, later admitted he followed the wrong path but it turned out to be a shortcut and we arrived into Esztergom sooner than expected.  As predicted by our guidebook, the Cathedral was very impressive.
 
At the campsite a hyperactive German child tried to engage us in conversation.  His father was very friendly towards us.  He directed us to the driest camping spot and invited us into his chalet for beer and dinner.  When he discovered that Rose was raised as a Muslim he cooked her an omelet rather than serving her the pork he had cooked.  He was convinced that it was 120km to Budapest and told us that we'd be better going somewhere else because of the Roma.  Despite his hospitality we were unconvinced by his directions and a bit uncomfortable with his views.  We thanked him for the meal and stuck with the map which had been right so far.  
 
We headed to a pub in the town without Steve, who was feeling unwell.  The bar was staffed by heavy metal fans and the walls were decorated with badly drawn pictures of rock legends.  One of the pictures looked like the German who made us dinner.  The bar man put on Dire Straights soon after we arrived.  An angry anarchist and old drunk who looked like The Greek from the Wire took a liking to Charlie.  On the way home Charlie remarked that it is never beautiful women who want to befriend you in a bar.   
 
The final day of cycling started with a struggle to find breakfast in the town.  I took the lead and guessed where to go.  We cycled along a canal past a museum and a school.  Luckily we arrived in Esztergom town centre.  It was a strange mix of beautiful old buildings and Soviet brutalism.  I quite liked it.  The first cafe we came to wasn't open.  The second only served cake, scones and coffee.  When the scones arrived they were tiny.  We persuaded the waitress to let us sit on the cafe terrace eating bread purchased from the bakery next door and left a generous tip.   
   
We cycled through the Hungarian countryside, stopping to take photographs.  It was hillier than Slovakia and marked by impressive castles.  After a few hours we crossed the Danube by ferry and began to look for places for lunch.  A local directed us to a diner.  We guessed at what to order and were served huge portions.  We then set off on the final part of our journey.    
 
As we approached Budapest the clouds closed over.  Charlie, optimistic as ever, said he thought it wouldn't rain.  Ten minutes later we raced to put on our waterproofs as another thunderstorm started.  We cycled along a waterfront sheltered by trees.  When we reached the end of the trees we hid under a railway bridge.  After waiting for 15 minutes hoping that the storm would pass we decided to press on.  We passed statues of women holding umbrellas, which seemed fitting, and neared the city centre.  
 
Budapest is divided into two parts: Buda and Pest.  We approached from Buda.  The most striking feature from our approach was an enormous church on the far side of a bridge.  Due to roadworks we had to wheel our bikes over the enormous bridge, stopping to take pictures and to send text messages saying we had made it.  Architecturally, the main streets of Pest reminded me of Paris but the city had the bohemian feel of Berlin.  We checked into the hostel and I phoned Kate.  I hadn't quite realised how tired I was and I spent most of the conversation trying to figure out how best to get hold of the keys for her flat that I had forgotten.  Soon afterwards I realised I would arrive back at the same time as she got home from work and it didn't matter.
 
We locked up our bikes in a car park staffed by a young man with frankly ridiculous hair.  We changed without bothering to shower and headed to a pub recommended by the staff of the hostel.  Our waiter was only slightly older than me but his bald head and large beard made him look not unlike Gandalf.  There were many celebratory toasts made, including one to me for having the most random knowledge of the group.  The next day I disassembled my bike with help from Darren and struggled to a cafe with all my gear.  After the meal I boarded a train to the airport.  The others were staying for a few days but my incredible cycle trip was over.
